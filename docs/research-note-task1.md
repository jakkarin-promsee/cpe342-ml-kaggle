## Models and results

**Journey.** Round 1 used the same 20% holdout for majority, logistic regression, random forest, and HGB on original versus combined features. Macro F₂ rose from **0.45152** (majority) and **0.67733** (original logistic regression) to **0.74157** (original forest) and **0.75285** (original HGB). Small development-only searches gave **0.76680** for the tuned combined forest, but choosing an HGB decision threshold on development folds raised its holdout score to **0.77684**. Baseline engineered features alone did not help consistently.

Round 2 compared LightGBM, XGBoost, and CatBoost on both feature sets, then tuned promising configurations and their thresholds. Their best thresholded holdout scores were **0.77847** (combined LightGBM), **0.77530** (combined XGBoost), and **0.77318** (combined CatBoost); original LightGBM scored **0.77762**. The small LightGBM feature-set gap after many holdout comparisons prompted Round 3: six paired outer folds comparing the finalists and a 38-feature ablation. Round 4 checked paired errors, a fixed 50/50 blend, and remaining threshold headroom before stopping broad search.

## OOF error audit

`src/task1/eda/analyze_top_model_errors_1.ipynb` reads the existing saved dataset and the two Round 4 OOF prediction repeats for the selected 38-feature procedure; it fits nothing and reads no Test rows. Repeat 1 has **11,846 false positives** among 59,091 legitimate accounts (**20.05%**) and **7,470 false negatives** among 31,725 cheaters (**23.55%**). In repeat 2, **10,148** of those false positives (**85.7%**) and **5,955** of those false negatives (**79.7%**) recur for the same accounts. The repeats reuse the same accounts and are not independent validation sets.

Descriptive slices show high missed-cheater rates for `reports_received` above 17 (**60.11%**, 1,058 cheaters; **59.07%** in repeat 2) and `account_age_days` from roughly 598 to 816 (**36.42%**, 5,200 cheaters; **33.96%** in repeat 2). Legitimate accounts with 4–8 reports have a **35.36%** false-positive rate (8,029 accounts; **36.97%** in repeat 2), and those with 49–105 friends have **34.32%** (6,439 accounts; **36.53%** in repeat 2). These are error concentrations, not proof of cause or a validated new feature. The notebook also visualizes probability margins, high-confidence mistakes, one- and two-variable slices, and cross-repeat slice stability. No feature set or model result changed.

An added raw-feature outlier audit applies 1.5×IQR fences to the 31 raw predictors on labeled Train. **17,532/90,816 rows (19.30%)** have at least one flag, dominated by `level_progression_speed` (**11,687 rows; 12.87%**). These statistical tails are not necessarily invalid records. Simple data-card-supported bounds on percentages, ratios, and nonnegative quantities find **zero** violating rows among observed values. In OOF repeat 1, **2,141/19,316 errors (11.08%)** are on IQR-flagged rows, while **5,369/19,316 (27.80%)** are within ±0.05 of that fold's threshold; repeat 2 gives **11.25%** and **27.93%**, respectively. The largest group is unflagged and farther than 0.05 from threshold (**12,318 errors**, 63.77% of repeat-1 errors). Flagged rows have *lower* error rates than unflagged rows away from the threshold, even within each true class. This does not quantify how much error is causally due to the model versus data; it argues against ordinary univariate IQR outliers as the dominant explanation.

## Round 5 — error-context exploration and model ablation

`build_eda_2.ipynb` reads raw Train/Test again, reproduces the prior 14 row-wise features, and saves exactly one new dataset, `eda_error_context_v2`, with eight additional row-wise candidates. **Context family:** `session_exposure_proxy` = account age × sessions/day; `reports_per_100_sessions` = 100 × reports/(exposure + 1); `friends_per_100_days` = 100 × friends/(age + 30); `reports_per_10_friends` = 10 × reports/(friends + 10). **Behavior family:** `reports_per_kd` = reports/(K/D + 1); `headshots_per_kd` = headshot percentage/(K/D + 1); `crosshair_smoothness_gap` = crosshair placement − aiming smoothness; `kill_input_consistency_gap` = kill consistency − input consistency. Missing inputs remain missing. No target or Test statistics enter these features.

The EDA joins the existing OOF predictions to labeled raw accounts, checks both repeats, and explores reports × friends cells. In the broad cells the mean model probability closely follows the observed cheater rate, while labels are often mixed: e.g. reports 9–14 and friends 50–105 contains **4,864** accounts with **58.2%** cheaters versus **57.2%** mean probability. There are **zero exact duplicate rows** across all 31 raw predictors. Far-from-boundary errors (`|probability − threshold| ≥ 0.2`) recur in repeat 2 at **99.2%** for FNs (1,102/1,111) and **99.1%** for FPs (2,517/2,541). This motivates checking new context and behavior signals but does not establish incorrect labels or an irreducible error floor.

`build_model_v3.ipynb` fixes the threshold at 0.400 and compares the same LightGBM settings on the same six outer folds. The baseline is rescored from its saved OOF probabilities at 0.400, so no baseline training is repeated. Scores are **mean macro F₂ over six folds**, with the same accounts reused across repeats:

| Feature set | Mean macro F₂, fixed 0.400 | Δ vs fixed baseline | Paired fold wins |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Original selected 38-feature baseline | 0.77705 | — | — |
| 38 + four context features | 0.77649 | −0.00056 | 1/6 |
| 38 + four behavior features | 0.77729 | +0.00024 | 4/6 |
| 38 + all eight new features | 0.77634 | −0.00071 | 2/6 |

The behavior candidate fixes **3,663** baseline mistakes across the repeated folds but creates **3,592** new ones; its modest gain is mainly in legitimate-class F₂ (**0.81897** versus **0.81824**), while cheater-class F₂ falls slightly (**0.73561** versus **0.73586**). Context and all-eight variants lose. These full-data models and their distinct `macro_F2_outer_cv6_fixed040` evaluation cards are saved, but that metric must not be conflated with the old inner-CV-tuned `macro_F2_outer_cv6` score of **0.77733**.

The follow-up `t1_lgbm_behavior_threshold_cv6_v3` uses the same 3-fold inner threshold selection as the old selected procedure. It scores **0.77690** mean outer macro F₂ against **0.77733** for the selected 38-feature model on the paired folds, a **−0.00042** difference and wins on only **2/6** folds. This reverses the tiny fixed-threshold gain, so the new behavior features do not establish a better model.

An analysis-only OOF blend check then compares fixed weights without refitting. The prior 50/50 pruned/original blend reproduces **0.778411** mean macro F₂. Pruned/behavior 50/50 gives **0.777993**; original/behavior 50/50 **0.777705**; equal thirds **0.778230**; 37.5% pruned + 37.5% original + 25% behavior **0.778460**. The last value exceeds the prior blend by only **0.000049** on the same reused folds after looking at several choices, so it is not credible evidence of a new best and no ensemble artifact is saved. Next the notebook checks three LightGBM capacity settings on the selected 38 features.

The fixed-threshold capacity check keeps the selected 38 features and compares three settings with the same folds and 0.400 threshold. Reducing `min_child_samples` from 100 to 30 scores **0.77664** (−0.00041 versus fixed baseline); increasing `n_estimators` from 450 to 700 scores **0.77548** (−0.00158). Reducing `num_leaves` from 63 to 31 **and** increasing `n_estimators` to 700 scores **0.77784** (+0.00079), winning **5/6** paired folds. This points to smoother, longer boosting as a more useful direction than the eight new row-wise features. The three saved models and evaluations use the fixed-threshold CV metric; the shallower candidate now receives a matched threshold-tuned follow-up.

The matched follow-up `t1_lgbm_shallower_more_trees_threshold_cv6_v3` scores **0.77748** mean macro F₂ versus **0.77733** for the old selected model, a **+0.00016** paired difference with **4/6** fold wins. The threshold-tuned comparison substantially shrinks the fixed-threshold gain. Fixed shallow blends from the saved OOF probabilities score **0.778508** for 50/50 pruned/shallow, **0.777935** for 50/50 original/shallow, **0.778567** for equal thirds, and **0.778651** for 37.5% pruned + 37.5% original + 25% shallow. The last exceeds the prior pruned/original blend (**0.778411**) by **0.000240**, after several exploratory blend checks on the same accounts. This is not a dependable new local best; no new blend model was saved.

The EDA notebook closes with a fold-separated nearest-neighbor diagnostic on sampled accounts, using five raw predictors (`reports_received`, `friend_network_size`, `account_age_days`, `crosshair_placement`, `kill_death_ratio`) and 25 neighbors from each validation fold's training rows. Preprocessing is fit on those training rows. Among **968 sampled persistent, far-from-threshold false negatives**, median support for their true cheater label is only **0.24** and just **4.9%** have a majority of neighbors labeled cheater. Among **1,050 sampled persistent, far-from-threshold false positives**, median support for their true legitimate label is **0.52** and **56.6%** have a legitimate-labeled majority. Stable far-from-threshold true negatives have **0.88** median support for their true label. This suggests the persistent false negatives look legitimate in these five dimensions, while false positives are more locally mixed. The limited feature space and sampled queries do not prove label errors, a causal mechanism, or an error floor.

## Insight and next step

Threshold selection helped more than the small original-versus-engineered feature gap. `reports_received`, `friend_network_size`, `account_age_days`, and `crosshair_placement` led validation permutation importance; this indicates reliance, not causation. Round 5 tested new error-context features, three capacity settings, matched threshold follow-ups, fixed blends, and a limited fold-separated local-neighborhood audit. The strongest standalone matched-protocol challenger, shallower LightGBM, improves by only **0.00016**; the largest examined ensemble mean is **0.778651**, only **0.000240** above the earlier blend on reused accounts. Keep the saved 38-feature model as the established standalone reference. **Deferred Task 1 follow-up:** inspect the persistent false positives with a broader but fold-local similarity measure or new behavioral evidence, then validate one specific hypothesis on paired folds. Do not treat the current neighbor audit as proof of label mistakes or as a competition score. A model change would need a larger, consistent paired-fold gain before replacing the current choice. Task 3 work takes priority for now.

## Class-imbalance hint check (v4)

`build_model_v4.ipynb` tested LightGBM's `class_weight="balanced"` on the established 38-feature set, using the same six outer folds (three folds, two repeats) and the same base model settings. The weighted model and its fixed-0.40-threshold CV evaluation are saved as `t1_lgbm_balanced_fixed040_cv6_v4`; its full-data fit is separate from the CV procedure estimate. At 0.40, mean macro F₂ falls from **0.77705** to **0.75974**. The class weights shift the probability scale, so this fixed-threshold score alone is not a fair final verdict. An exploratory sweep of 0.40–0.60 on the **same OOF rows** reaches at most **0.77765** near 0.53, only **0.00032** above the selected model's properly inner-threshold-tuned outer-CV score of **0.77733**. That sweep is optimistic because it selects and scores the threshold on the same labels; it is not an independent weighted-model estimate. There is no evidence of a material class-weight gain worth a costly nested threshold follow-up or SMOTE search now. Freeze the selected 38-feature model; no new Task 1 Kaggle candidate is recommended.
